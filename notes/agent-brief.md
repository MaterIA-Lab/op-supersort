## Goal in one line

Find a genuinely new, architecture-aware mechanism that sorts a practically relevant workload ≥1.3× faster than the fastest competitive reference on fresh inputs, with correct output. ≥1.5× on an important class must be reported to the owner before broad exploration continues. Tuning, patching or dispatching between known sorters does not count. See [GOAL.md](../GOAL.md).

## Do not retry

- **Promoting the random-update gain against the old reference set alone.** The same 128 MiB inputs gave about 2.0–2.25× against the older controls and 1.004–1.137× against cache-buffered merging. A 64.13 MiB real-duration replay with 0.047–0.180% median edit density and roughly 25,000 ranks of typical displacement also missed the gate. [Decision experiment](../runs/2026-11-24-key-correction-decision/README.md)
- **Short updates on the measured small Blitz ranking.** At 2.44 MiB, the per-window median edited displacement was typically around 180 ranks. All 48 comparisons missed the 77% time limit despite exact minimum unchanged-row writes. Benchmark larger traces with substantial typical displacement before drawing conclusions about sparse ranking updates generally. [Lichess Blitz replay](../runs/2026-11-23-lichess-rating-replay/README.md)
- **Treating all tracked corrections as random relocations.** With new keys taken within 256 ranks of the old position, upstream DeltaSort took 1.643–2.263 ms and the direct-span prototype took 1.888–2.399 ms. Keep this separate from the registered uniform-replacement class. [Controls](../runs/2026-11-23-key-correction-spans/README.md#development-controls)
- **Using only ESM as the comparison on widely shifted real batches.** The FIDE pair gave about half the unchanged-row write requests and 1.93-2.01× against the fastest ESM control, while the fastest reference overall was Rust's unstable full sort. The latter gave 1.178-1.230×. [Timing table](../runs/2026-11-23-fide-rating-updates/README.md#timing-results)
- **64 saved-row cuts plus interleaved scalar walkers under the registered scratch contract.** Ordinary cycles became 2.38-2.47x faster end to end, but total remained 7.4-10.1% slower than streaming gather/copy-back on both 128 MiB uniform32 seeds. 216 calls verified; no holdout claim. Lower auxiliary payload storage is an inferred property, not a measured process-memory win or permission to change the primary class. [Run](../runs/2026-11-22-checkpoint-cycles/README.md)
- **Scalar traversal of prefix membership masks for 32-row stable sorts.** Ordinary uint8 keys plus uint32 payloads: 225.1-225.3 ns/group versus 36.6-39.4 for a direct AVX2 16-bit tag network. All 240 calls verified, zero of two primary cells passed. This changes the mechanism from numeric-rank inversion but still loses substantially. [Run](../runs/2026-11-22-bitplane-sort/README.md)
- **Tile-and-sort payload permutation** (destination tiles of 256 KiB, descriptors sorted by source with vqsort). Materialization took 1.67–2.14× as long as a plain vqsort gather. [Run](../runs/2026-11-12-blocked-permutation/README.md)
- **Counting-based source grouping inside each destination window.** A plan with 4,096-row source bands and 65,536-slot windows took 118.93–123.54 ms to materialize the 128 MiB primary output, compared with 71.66–72.39 ms for the best indirect reference. [Run](../runs/2026-11-14-record-permutation/README.md)
- **Page remapping with 4 KiB extents on the measured WSL2 setup.** Mapping and first read cost 64 ms more than copying 128 MiB. The measured candidate used a 64 KiB cutoff, the smallest profitable size tested there. [Pilot](../runs/2026-11-12-page-remapped-merge/summary.md)
- **Fresh Windows placeholder remap against a resident destination.** With 2 MiB views over 256 MiB, remap took under 0.7 ms, and a first read of one word per 4 KiB page took roughly 83 ms. The first-read cost also dominated the 64 KiB primary. [Run](../runs/2026-11-14-page-remap/README.md)
- **Windows producer placement using a new pagefile-backed section.** With roughly 98% of bytes reused, assembly and first read plus writing both new batches took 277–540 ms across the primary pairs. The best direct pipeline took 140–233 ms. [Run](../runs/2026-11-16-producer-page-placement/README.md)
- **Fresh Linux producer placement with new `memfd` batches.** Both shifted mapping variants missed the 77% time limit in all eight holdout cells despite reusing about 98% of the output. Writing the two input batches took 80–84% of elapsed time. Eager population moved 3.6–8.3 ms out of the first read, with changes of −2.2 to +1.1 ms in the end-to-end median. [Run](../runs/2026-11-16-linux-producer-placement/README.md)
- **AVX2 rank inversion through byte-position shifts for four packed key–payload records.** It still took 30–32% longer than compare-exchange on the 128 KiB controls. [Run](../runs/2026-11-14-four-row-ranks/README.md)
- **Three-byte intermediates justified by bandwidth savings alone.** At eight workers, even two favorable sequential DRAM transfers could only support an estimated 1.086–1.178× after charging both conversions. [Screen](../runs/2026-11-15-compressed-key-bandwidth/README.md)
- **Dedicated bounded lookahead for random gathers of 128-byte rows.** Two direct writers won both primary seeds with cached stores and again with streaming stores available. [Benchmark](../runs/2026-11-15-smt-gather-helper/README.md)
- **Predicting reusable pages from long source slices alone.** Bursty runs had about 88% of bytes in full source pages, but only 2.7–6.9% aligned with the destination.
- **Choosing placement offsets for the measured monthly airport pairs.** Across all 36 pairs, 99.21–99.95% of rows were in the shared interval and no same-source slice exceeded 17 rows, below 32 rows per page. Changing either input's placement or the output offset cannot fix the short slices. [Diagnostic](../runs/2026-11-16-flight-overlap/README.md)
- **Single-phase replacements inside vqsort** for high-entropy uint32 keys, and **dispatching between known sorters**. See [prior research](prior-research.md) and [GOAL.md](../GOAL.md).

## Controls and references to beat

- **Cache-buffered extract–sort–merge.** Include 32 and 128 KiB original blocks. Compact away deleted rows while filling the scratch queue. Merge survivors and corrected records directly into the original array before the next refill. The queue needs at most one additional row per edit. A buffered reference led all 48 flight-duration comparisons and all four 128 MiB random checks. [Implementation](../prototypes/agents/key-correction-decision/README.md)
- **Tracked key corrections in a sorted array.** Race both directions of bulk extract-sort-merge with identical edit preprocessing and overlap-safe copies. Include interval-limited variants, upstream DeltaSort and both Rust full sorts. For bounded rating keys include counting with dense tie ordering and prefetched gather and copy-back. Preserve the original array until timing begins for all methods. [Synthetic benchmark](../runs/2026-11-23-key-correction-spans/README.md) · [Flight replay](../runs/2026-11-23-flight-key-corrections/README.md) · [FIDE pair](../runs/2026-11-23-fide-rating-updates/README.md)
- **In-place 128-byte records with workspace available.** vqsort uint64 key/index tags, gather both row cache lines with 32-row prefetch and AVX2 NT stores, fence, then sequential AVX2 NT copy-back and fence. It beat cached gather/memcpy, x86-simd-sort tags with the same movement, direct pdqsort and the 64-checkpoint design on both large uniform32 cells. IPS4o remains unmeasured. [Medians and code](../runs/2026-11-22-checkpoint-cycles/README.md)
- **Stable 32-row byte-key groups.** Batch a direct AVX2 bitonic network over uint16 `(key << 5) | slot` tags, then materialize keys and uint32 payloads once. It beat pinned libraries using uint32 tags and full-domain stable counting in all six cells. It already avoids intermediate payload movement. [Source and medians](../runs/2026-11-22-bitplane-sort/README.md)
- **Records, out of place.** Argsort with vqsort or x86-simd-sort on packed `(key, index)` words, then gather with software prefetch 32 rows ahead (both cache lines for 128-byte rows). It won all 48 paired repetitions of the blocked probe. The paired median speedup over the fastest reference without prefetch was 1.051–1.235×. IPS⁴o and a record-oriented SIMD merge are still unmeasured. [Reference timings](../runs/2026-11-12-blocked-permutation/summary.md)
- **Merging two sorted runs:** fastest of a scalar two-finger merge, a galloping bulk merge and a hybrid that bulk-copies the non-overlapping prefix and suffix. Also report the hybrid writing into a pre-touched destination, which is a different memory contract.
- **Assembling disjoint key ranges.** Include the endpoint check plus two bulk copies in both fresh and resident modes. The eight GH Archive cases preserve real row counts and the verified two-slice order, with synthetic 128-byte records. [Measurements](../runs/2026-11-12-gharchive-disjoint-merge/summary.md)
- **Adjustable offsets while producing both batches.** Two endpoint searches find the outer stretches. The quickest direct method in all 24 Linux cells was a fused writer that loads each row once and uses cached stores to write it to its batch and the final output. Include setup, batch materialization and first read. [Flight geometry](../runs/2026-11-16-flight-overlap/README.md) · [Linux producer timings](../runs/2026-11-16-linux-producer-placement/README.md)
- **Sequential copy of the same bytes.** An empirical proxy for headroom. Allocation and cache state differ from a gather, so use measured candidate times for performance claims. [Pilot timing contract](../runs/2026-11-12-record-permutation/README.md)
- **Two-thread gathers.** Split the final destination into contiguous halves. Try on-worker prefetch with cached stores and non-temporal stores, keeping CPU placement equal. The streaming split was fastest in all four large diagnostic cases. [Measurements](../runs/2026-11-15-smt-gather-helper/README.md)
- **Windows primitive controls.** Use AVX2 non-temporal stores with a fence and measure the first consumer sweep and teardown. Report fresh, precommitted and already-touched outputs as distinct contracts.
- **Packed four-record groups.** A direct three-stage AVX2 compare-exchange network and the comparison-mask shuffle lookup both preserve ties using source slot tags. Batch the references just as the candidate is batched. [Controls](../runs/2026-11-14-four-row-ranks/README.md)

## Contracts used so far

- **BTS duration reconciliation:** fixed 525,370 complete January 2024 reports, scheduled versus actual elapsed minutes, local scheduled departure buckets, 128-byte modeled records. A dense key-by-ID state supplies old keys and is updated inside the timer. ID mode also locates positions inside timing. Primary ID mode with copy reset plus supplied-position and cache-flush controls. [Protocol](../runs/2026-11-24-key-correction-decision/protocol.md)
- **Lichess rating refresh:** 19,982 fixed-roster accounts, pre-game Blitz ratings observed from the January 2015 archive, modeled 128-byte rows and original positions supplied. Twelve fixed start times with 60 and 600 second windows, copy reset and CLFLUSH/MFENCE, nine methods, full-row checks. [Lichess protocol](../runs/2026-11-23-lichess-rating-replay/protocol.md)
- **FIDE rating update:** fixed membership across June and July 2024, real ratings, dense IDs ordered by FIDE identifier and synthetic 128-byte rows. All 29,726 changed ratings at known positions in one batch. Copy-reset and flushed regimes on one CPU. Raw personal records and replay fixture stay local. [Protocol](../runs/2026-11-23-fide-rating-updates/protocol.md)
- **Flight timestamp replay:** 32,735 rows from the pinned OpenIntro sample with 128-byte modeled records and corrections to reported local departure minutes. The caller supplies original positions in actual departure order. The first full weeks of four months yield 28 daily batches. Time complete calls with copy reset alone and with CLFLUSH/MFENCE after reset. Check all rows against the next day's starting snapshot. [Protocol](../runs/2026-11-23-flight-key-corrections/protocol.md)
- **Unapplied key corrections:** original array sorted by `(u32 key, persistent u32 id)`, 128-byte rows, original position supplied per unique edit in arbitrary order. One thread returns all rows at the same address, with planning, scratch lifetime, corrections and copies timed. Main validation is 1,024 or 2,048 random replacements at 128 or 256 MiB, about 0.098%. Cache lines flushed before large calls. Reserve the timings for this contract, including the ability to search the intact original key order. [Protocol](../runs/2026-11-23-key-correction-spans/protocol.md)
- **Saved-row cycle screen:** stable in-place 128-byte rows, mutable caller array, N uint64 tags and N payload rows supplied as reusable workspace to all methods. Allocation excluded equally; complete tag extraction/sort/permutation/copy-back timed. Primary N=1,048,576 uniform32, seeds 48071/65827; eight-key large controls and N=8,192 resident controls. Fixed 64 checkpoint budget, arbitrary lengths supported. CPU 8, CLFLUSH/MFENCE on work and both scratch arrays for large cells. Five measured rounds after warm-up.
- **32-row bitplane screen:** immutable SoA uint8 keys and uint32 payloads, separate reusable ordinary output, 65,536-group primary and eight-key controls, 128-group resident control. CPU 8; explicit CLFLUSH/MFENCE on input and output for large cells; seven measured rounds after warm-up, exact stable-output verification. Seeds 104729/130363 are development inputs for this candidate, not holdouts.
- **Records:** 64- or 128-byte rows aligned to 64 bytes, `uint32_t` key, unique origin ID, immutable input, fresh contiguous output, 1,048,576 and 4,194,304 records, uniform32 and low12 keys, SplitMix64 seeds.
- **Merge:** two ascending runs of 128-byte records with unique `uint64_t` timestamps, inputs in page-aligned read-only `memfd` views, read-only contiguous output. Timing ends after a first sequential scan of one word per cache line; teardown is reported separately.
- **Three-output producer:** two sorted mutable buffers of 128-byte records, copied into new read-only batches with a contiguous stable merged view. The original buffers can be reused. Time output reservation, materialization, protection and the first scan. Keep output verification and teardown outside the total.
- **Common:** one thread pinned to logical CPU 4, a 64 MiB eviction buffer touched before each trial, one warm-up plus 3–5 measured repetitions with rotated method order, every output verified outside timing, fresh seeds (104729, 130363) for candidates after development seeds (271, 1601).
- **2026-11-15 primitive probes:** persistent pinned workers, pre-touched reusable outputs, 128 MiB eviction on large cases, one warm-up and seven measured rounds. The compressed-key probe times individual phases. The helper probe times permutation using fixed indices with SMT and two-core placements. [Bandwidth protocol](../runs/2026-11-15-compressed-key-bandwidth/protocol.md) · [Gather protocol](../runs/2026-11-15-smt-gather-helper/protocol.md)

Summaries, oldest first:

- **2026-11-12-smoke** (setup): The prior local runner still compiles and runs on Windows with GCC 16.1 and sorts 1,000 uint32 keys correctly. Not an evaluation of the goal.
- **2026-11-12-repo-reset** (setup): Judged the original goal (25% faster in 90% of a large benchmark matrix) very unlikely to be reached, replaced it with an open search for new mechanisms and archived the prior research.
- **2026-11-12-record-permutation** (diagnostic): Gathering the payload takes 51–68% of an indirect sort of 64- and 128-byte records, so a permutation as cheap as a sequential copy would give about 1.3–1.4×. Enough room to try a candidate, with little margin.
- **2026-11-12-blocked-permutation** (failed): Rewriting and sorting descriptors and then moving the records in cache-sized tiles took 1.67–2.14× as long as the original gather phase. The probe measured those costs together. Gather with software prefetch won all 48 paired repetitions.
- **2026-11-12-page-remapped-merge** (diagnostic): Mapping pages instead of copying them pays off from 64 KiB extents upwards (4 KiB extents lose). In a merge only page-aligned slices qualify, which was 37.5% of the output when two runs overlap at the edges and 3–7% for bursty runs.
- **2026-11-12-page-remapped-merge-candidate** (promising): Mapping aligned input pages into the output beat the best fresh-output merge of two sorted runs of 128-byte records by 1.33–1.36×, and by 2.67× when 75% of the output was mapped. It lost to a merge into an already-touched buffer (0.36–0.74×) and regressed on bursty and interleaved runs (0.82–0.86×). Synthetic data only.
- **2026-11-12-gharchive-page-coverage** (diagnostic): All eight registered archive pairs had disjoint event-time ranges. Between 48.25% and nearly 100% of the modeled bytes can be remapped, so these inputs measure assembly of preordered ranges and leave overlap untested.
- **2026-11-12-gharchive-disjoint-merge** (promising): On eight disjoint pairs with batch sizes and order derived from GH Archive, remapping beat every fresh-output reference. It beat the resident-buffer references in the two cases where almost all pages could be mapped. Real overlapping arrivals remain untested.
- **2026-11-14-record-permutation** (failed): Grouping reads by source band took about 23% longer on the primary batch. Preparing the groups and moving rows together took longer than reading ahead in the original layout.
- **2026-11-14-page-remap** (failed): For 64 KiB blocks, Windows remapping and a first read took 2.21–2.54× the time of streaming copy and a first read. The first access to the new virtual addresses dominated even when larger blocks made the remap calls fast.
- **2026-11-14-four-row-ranks** (failed): Computing ranks and inverting them in AVX2 registers took 5–37% longer than the fastest direct SIMD reference on the primary batches. The extra work still cost time in the cache-resident control.
- **2026-11-14-taxi-page-coverage** (diagnostic): Only one of twelve weekly pairs overlapped in pickup time. Four rows of that pair fell inside the overlap, and choosing the output offset did not increase its page reuse.
- **2026-11-15-compressed-key-bandwidth** (diagnostic): With eight workers, a transfer-only estimate including conversion reached 1.086–1.178×. All four primary cases missed the 1.30× gate before any sorting work was charged.
- **2026-11-15-smt-gather-helper** (failed): The best helper took 180–182 ms on two threads of one core. Giving each thread half the output took 102–114 ms for the same 256 MiB permutation.
- **2026-11-16-flight-overlap** (diagnostic): The 36 monthly airport pairs had more than 99% of rows in the shared time interval. Even the longest single-source stretch was shorter than one page. Selecting each input's alignment nearly doubled median reusable coverage for lightly overlapping weekly batches.
- **2026-11-16-producer-page-placement** (failed): With 2% overlap, independent placement doubled reusable coverage from 49% to about 98%. The Windows implementation took 1.84–2.43 times as long as the best direct reference when producing both batches and the merged result.
- **2026-11-16-linux-producer-placement** (failed): Adjusted page placement was 1.231–1.280× as fast as the best direct writer across eight fresh edge-overlap cases on two Linux runners. Every case missed the 1.30× target, and building the two batches took 80–84% of the remapping time.
- **2026-11-22-bitplane-sort** (failed): Keeping the sort in bit masks took 5.71-6.15 times the direct AVX2 network's time on random byte keys. All 240 timed calls were correct, but neither primary seed approached the speed target.
- **2026-11-22-checkpoint-cycles** (failed): Interleaved paths reduced the full sort from 126-129 ms with ordinary cycles to 52-53 ms, but streaming gather plus copy-back still won at 48-49 ms. Both primary inputs missed the speed target.
- **2026-11-23-key-correction-spans** (promising): Applying a small batch of key corrections to a sorted array ran 1.81–2.10× as fast as the fastest measured reference on fresh 128 and 256 MiB cases. The prototype wrote about half as many unchanged rows. DeltaSort was faster when corrections stayed close to their original ranks.
- **2026-11-23-flight-key-corrections** (failed): The direct-span prototype took 29-31% longer than the fastest reference in the median day of a 28-day replay. The sample contained 32,735 flights. DeltaSort was the fastest reference in 50 of 56 day and cache combinations.
